# Design specification — Academy vision and course experience

## Selected direction

Multilingual Academy vision + visible course evidence.

The page should make the future feel worth building before it explains the mechanics. Tabor's teaching image keeps the Academy vision human. The owner-selected course-suite composition then establishes scope inside the course section; the original Digital Creative 101 interface establishes that a real learning system is taking shape; the Home Connection photograph makes the live one-on-one path human and distinct from the course.

## Page sequence and job

1. **Academy vision:** Tabor's teaching image behind a multilingual, Gospel-centered vision and the intended experience of confidence.
2. **Learning experience:** full computer/course/workbook/audio/field-kit suite on the left; Digital Creative 101 purpose and course-plan explanation on the right.
3. **Course-design facts:** four concrete commitments before the detailed interface.
4. **Course interior:** original SVG on the left; the reusable Academy lesson pattern on the right.
5. **Course structure:** a Start Here block plus six Digital Creative 101 modules, the lesson group inside each module, and the evidence each module produces.
6. **Home Connection:** live one-on-one guidance, supported by a user-requested generated working-call image with Tabor centered and looking into the camera; explicitly not an Academy course.

## Copy intent

- **Vision:** “A future where every missionary is confident in digital media and digital marketing.”
- **Mission:** make practical media and technology training clear, useful, guided, and shaped for missionary life.
- **Intended experience:** digital confidence—knowing what to do next and being able to use the learning on current ministry work.
- **Truth boundary:** the Academy is being built; Digital Creative 101 is a visual course plan; the interface is not a live learning platform.
- **Home Connection boundary:** a live, one-on-one working session, not a course or guided lab.

## Reusable Academy lesson pattern

1. Teaching block — one skill or decision in 5–10 minutes.
2. Guided task — immediate practice on ministry work.
3. Knowledge check — a short test of the core idea.
4. Download — a reusable guide, template, checklist, or setup card.
5. Repeat — the same lesson rhythm across the course.
6. Live review — work and one question brought to feedback.
7. Capstone — lesson work combined into a final project and revision plan.

## Digital Creative 101 module structure

The source pattern comes from Tabor's complete local Think Media / VRA Foundations archive: 42 lesson files across six modules. Missionary Media uses the hierarchy—not Think Media's protected labels, scripts, or sequence—to build an original field-update system. The complete mapping is recorded in `THINK-MEDIA-STRUCTURE-MAP.md`.

1. Purpose — three lessons; one-sentence communication brief.
2. Plan — four lessons; one-page recording plan.
3. Capture — five lessons; organized raw-media folder.
4. Send — five lessons; captioned 60-second update ready for review.
5. Learn — three lessons; one-page performance review.
6. Repeat — four lessons; reviewed update and repeatable system.

Start Here adds four setup lessons before the 24 guided module lessons.

## Visual system

- Navy `#07111f` frame and ink.
- Cream `#f5f0e8` page and pale work surfaces.
- Cobalt `#005be8` active state.
- Sky `#00c4f9` small signal accents.
- Coral `#f63600` reserved for capstone emphasis.
- Source Sans 3 for page UI; Arial-family fallback inside the standalone SVG.
- Opening composition: the owner-selected Tabor teaching image remains the full-width hero.
- Course-experience composition: the transparent course, computer, workbook, audio, and field-kit image sits beside the Digital Creative 101 explanation.
- Course interior: straight rules, modest radii, and an original deterministic interface rather than generic course thumbnails.
- Home Connection: local generated image `home-connection-working-call-v4.jpg` on the right inside a warm cream card. Tabor is centered and looking toward the phone on its tripod—the live-call camera—with the laptop, notebook, and headphones visible; the live-help message remains on the left.

## Accessibility and responsive intent

- The mobile-specific hero crop keeps the vision copy readable while preserving Tabor as the human anchor.
- Both major images have descriptive alternative text and adjacent HTML that carries the meaning.
- Headings preserve logical order and status language is textual, not color-only.
- The page itself has no horizontal overflow; the detailed course interface becomes a clearly bounded horizontal inspection surface on small screens.
- Reduced-motion users retain all content without depending on hover or animation.
