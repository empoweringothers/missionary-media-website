# Research — Academy Digital Creative course showcase

## Decision questions

1. What evidence makes an in-development course feel concrete without pretending it is already operating?
2. What learning sequence can combine creator education, filmmaking fundamentals, and technical-training rigor without copying another curriculum?
3. Which details belong inside the interface image, and which need prose beside it?

## Audience and task evidence

The current internal course blueprint says every lesson should be short enough to begin on a difficult week and should leave behind something useful. Its working pattern is 5–10 minutes of teaching, a guided task, a check, and a reusable download. It also reserves live review and capstone work for feedback rather than treating a library of files as the product. Local source: `MissionaryMedia_Brain/04 Strategy/Jeff Rapp Prep/2026-07-17/course-experience-blueprint/course-blueprint-2026-07-17.md`, accessed 2026-08-26.

The product architecture says Home Connection is the current guided one-on-one proof path while the course is future delivery architecture. Local source: `MissionaryMedia_Brain/04 Strategy/Jeff Rapp Prep/2026-07-17/home-connection-offer-and-founder-fit-2026-07-17.md`, accessed 2026-08-26.

### Intro to digital media reference

The supplied course-structure overview pairs 5–10 minute lectures with a practical workshop, a quiz, and a final project. Its useful principle is consistent lesson grammar. We reject the draft badge/certification language because no competency standard or reviewer system has been validated. ### CompTIA A+ V15

The current official Core 1 and Core 2 objectives organize coverage into explicit domains, use scenario verbs such as install, configure, troubleshoot, and document, and mix multiple-choice with performance-based questions. The current Core 1 document also recommends hands-on experience and explicitly retains a troubleshooting methodology as a useful competency standard. Transferable principle: name the domain, state the observable task, let the learner perform it, then verify it. We will not imply CompTIA affiliation, exam readiness, accreditation, or certification. ## Adjacent analogues

A technical field guide is a stronger analogue than a course box: it names the task, shows the procedure, supplies a reference card, and includes a completion check. The interface preview therefore behaves like a working lesson surface, not like ecommerce packaging.

## Visual and editorial patterns

- Use one large interface composition with a visible course map, active lesson, time budget, objective, task, check, download, and capstone link.
- Make the lesson title a verb-led production decision, not a clever headline.
- Use restrained system labels and numerical hierarchy; avoid floating mockup cards, fake avatars, fake progress, stars, trophies, and AI-generated photography.
- Place the explanation beside the image so it can state why every element exists.

## Interaction and motion patterns

The preview is static. Existing page-level scroll behavior may remain, but no essential curriculum evidence depends on reveal animation. Reduced-motion users receive the same content.

## Reusable implementation candidates

None. The interface is small enough to author directly with project HTML, CSS, and SVG. Importing a component library would add provenance and maintenance cost without improving the concept.

## Themes and contradictions

- Specificity builds authority; unsupported market percentages do not.
- A visible workflow is stronger evidence than a cover image.
- Live feedback belongs in the learning loop, but Home Connection itself is not the course.
- Certification structure is useful; certification language is not yet truthful.

## Design principles

1. Show the work, not the promise.
2. Quantify the course design, not unproven demand.
3. One lesson, one visible outcome.
4. Make practice and review as prominent as teaching.
5. Keep current help and future curriculum visibly separate.

## Anti-references

- Generic course-box stacks: they show packaging rather than instruction.
- Founder-at-a-desk thumbnails repeated as curriculum evidence: they show a person, not the learner experience.
- Fake dashboards with arbitrary progress, avatar clusters, completion badges, or testimonial numbers: they imply operating data that does not exist.
- Certification-like seals: they imply standards and authority not yet defined.

## Unknowns

- Final lesson video, worksheet, and submission technology.
- Final live-review cadence and cohort format.
- Final Church IT domains and reviewer ownership.

These unknowns do not prevent an honest course-structure preview.
